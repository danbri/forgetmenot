That beach dog-fouling sign, explained

A sign on a North Somerset beach (Weston-super-Mare area) cites the Anti-social Behaviour, Crime and Policing Act 2014 and a “Public Spaces Protection Order” (PSPO). Here is the actual chain of law behind it — and an illustrative map of PSPO boundaries from the handful of councils that publish them as open data.

Tier 1 · Primary Act of Parliament
Anti-social Behaviour, Crime and Policing Act 2014 (ukpga/2014/12), Part 4, Chapter 2 (ss. 59–75) creates PSPOs. s.59 = the open power for a local authority to prohibit or require "specified things" in the restricted area — dog controls (carry a bag, leads, exclusion) are a common use of that power, not a named category. The Act special-cases only two activity types: alcohol (ss. 62–63) and restricting a public right of way over a highway (ss. 64–65). Breach is a criminal offence (ss. 67–68) — a Fixed Penalty Notice or prosecution and a fine. Max 3 years, renewable. PSPOs consolidated earlier regimes: Dog Control Orders (Clean Neighbourhoods and Environment Act 2005), alcohol DPPOs and gating orders.
Tier 2 · National Statutory Instrument
The Anti-social Behaviour, Crime and Policing Act 2014 (Publication of Public Spaces Protection Orders) Regulations 2014 (uksi/2014/2591), made by the Secretary of State and on the legislation.gov.uk SI register. It governs how a council must publish a PSPO.
Tier 3 · The PSPO itself — a LOCAL order
The order on the sign is made by the council (e.g. North Somerset). It is NOT a Statutory Instrument: not made by a Minister, not laid before Parliament, not in the legislation.gov.uk SI series. It is subordinate legislation in substance (legally binding, criminal sanctions) but administratively a local order, published by the council under uksi/2014/2591.

The teaching point: the SI (Tier 2) only says how to publish. The order that actually bans dog-fouling on your beach (Tier 3) is a local order, not an SI — which is why you will never find “the North Somerset beach PSPO” on legislation.gov.uk.

England & Wales only. The PSPO provisions of the 2014 Act do not extend to Scotland or Northern Ireland — they run their own anti-social-behaviour regimes. The sample below happens to be all-English: no Welsh council was found publishing PSPO boundaries as open data at build time (which does not mean Welsh councils have none).

Why isn't a PSPO a Statutory Instrument?

An SI is delegated legislation made by a Minister under power delegated by Parliament, numbered in the uksi/ series and (usually) laid before Parliament. A PSPO is made by a local authority under s.59 of the 2014 Act, following local consultation; it is not laid before Parliament and is not numbered as an SI. Both are “subordinate” / “secondary” legislation in the broad sense (rules made under a parent Act), but only the Minister-made instrument is a Statutory Instrument. The two SIs you will find on the register here are the national publication regulations (uksi/2014/2591), not the orders themselves.

Every polygon is sourced. Each council's PSPO boundaries are fetched from that council's own open-data service; the popup separates the sourced facts (council, name, source URL) from the interpreted legal-basis chain.

Boundaries are server-side / display generalised — not authoritative for legal extent. Where a source is in British National Grid (EPSG:27700) it was reprojected to WGS84 for display.
