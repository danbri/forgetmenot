The UK's legislated geographies

Every layer is a geography defined in law — a boundary you can only know by reading the instrument that draws it. Most are drawn by secondary legislation (a statutory instrument or Order made under an Act of Parliament — constituencies, marine zones, council areas, the COVID tiers). But it's a genuine mix, not uniform: some are statutory notifications or declarations made directly under a primary Act (SSSIs under the Wildlife & Countryside Act 1981, nature reserves under the 1949 Act — not statutory instruments); some councils trace to a primary Act (London boroughs → London Government Act 1963); and one is an international designation (Ramsar — no UK instrument at all). Most are also devolved, so the instrument differs by nation. Each feature's popup names the exact instrument and traces it to the primary Act on legislation.gov.uk.

Why is this “secondary legislation drawing a map”?

A statutory instrument can do more than write rules — it can draw a boundary. Constituency boundaries are set by an Order in Council; each marine zone, National Park, National Landscape / AONB and National Scenic Area is created by its own designation order under a nation-specific Act. The COVID case is the starkest — and the most divergent: England's tier schedule listed which councils sat in which tier, but Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland each ran an entirely separate legal regime. The legislation is the map, but there were four maps.

The legal hierarchy — primary Act vs secondary Order

Most boundaries here are drawn by secondary legislation (an Order or SI), whose power to draw comes from a primary Act — but not all: some are statutory notifications/declarations made directly under a primary Act (SSSIs, nature reserves), and Ramsar is an international listing with no UK instrument. Each popup shows the level that applies. Examples of the two-level chain:

Westminster constituencies: drawn by the Parliamentary Constituencies Order 2023 (SI 2023/1230, an Order in Council) under the Parliamentary Constituencies Act 1986 (c. 56, primary).

Local government: boundaries drawn by Orders, but the council itself is authorised by a primary Act that differs by nation — the Local Government Act 1972 (England, c. 70); the London Government Act 1963 (c. 33) for the 32 London boroughs; the Local Democracy, Economic Development and Construction Act 2009 (c. 20, which set up England's boundary commission); Local Government (Wales) Act 1994 (c. 19); Local Government etc. (Scotland) Act 1994 (c. 39); Local Government Act (NI) 2014 (c. 8). Each LAD popup cites the right one for its nation.

Honesty: Northern Ireland has no national parks, so it is genuinely absent from that one layer (stated above, not silently dropped). Scotland's COVID protection levels are transcribed from the gov.scot allocation review in force on 2 Dec 2020; Wales and NI are shown as a single national fill because no per-area choropleth was sourced for that date — their popups link the relevant nation's own regulations rather than inventing per-area data. Boundaries are server-side generalised for web display — not authoritative for legal extent. No UK Parliament API data is used on this page.
