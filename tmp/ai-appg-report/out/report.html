AI-related APPG officers: donations and commercial connections

Cross-reference of officers of the three AI-related All-Party Parliamentary Groups against the Register of Members' Financial Interests (Commons + Lords) and the Electoral Commission donations register.

Run date: 24 May 2026 · APPG cache edition: 260413 (fetched 3 May 2026) · Commons RMFI snapshot: registerId 799, published 18 May 2026 · full paper trail below.

Summary

Twelve people serve as officers (Chair, Co-Chair, Vice Chair or Officer) across the three All-Party Parliamentary Groups whose subject matter directly concerns artificial intelligence, blockchain, or data and emerging technologies in the current parliament (register edition 260413, published April 2026). This report cross-references their personal financial interests and the Electoral Commission's donations register to identify donors and commercial counterparties that connect more than one of them, and to find the wider set of recipients that those same donors fund.

Headline findings

  1. Anthony Watson, a Labour-supporting individual donor, is both the largest declared individual donor to Dawn Butler MP (Vice Chair, APPG on AI — £46,700 across seven gifts) and a recurring donor to Peter Kyle MP, the Secretary of State for Science, Innovation and Technology — the Cabinet minister responsible for AI policy (£16,000 across three gifts). Same individual donor, the AI APPG leadership and the AI Cabinet portfolio.
  2. UKUS Crypto Alliance, a small US-UK crypto trade body, paid for overseas visits to Washington DC in late 2025 / early 2026 for two of our twelve officers — Lord Ranger of Northwood (Vice Chair, APPG on AI) and Baroness Uddin (Officer, APPG for Data and Emerging Technologies). Their first trips overlapped in date. No Commons MP has declared a visit paid for by the same body.
  3. Big Innovation Centre, the secretariat for the current APPG on AI, has historically funded MPs' attendance at the AI Everything conference in Dubai through its Middle East arm (£6,500 for Damien Moore MP and £7,000 for Stephen Metcalfe MP, both April 2019, both then sitting as APPG Blockchain / APPG AI Chair respectively). The current 12 officers have not declared anything from the Centre, but the prior pattern is on the register.
  4. Lord Holmes of Richmond (Officer, APPG for Data and Emerging Technologies) has 45 currently registered paid roles in the Lords Register, including non-executive directorships at the Avalanche Foundation (blockchain) and consultancies on AI and blockchain at Simmons & Simmons, Submer Technologies (data-centre cooling for AI) and others. This is a substantial commercial footprint in the AI/crypto sector by an officer of the APPG for Data and Emerging Technologies.
  5. Lord Clement-Jones (Co-Chair, APPG on AI) is a paid consultant to DLA Piper UK LLP, which has been a recurring corporate donor to the Labour Party (£100k+ across 2007–2022). No direct payment from DLA Piper to Clement-Jones is on the EC donations register — his retainer is properly declared on his personal Lords RMFI entry instead.
Read this before quoting any figure. Of these five findings, (1), (2), (4) and (5) are robustly anchored to the relevant registers and the underlying API records are linked in the evidence drawers. Finding (3) concerns prior officers, not the current twelve, and is contextual rather than direct. See Caveats for the full list of limitations — particularly that the AI APPG secretariat funding for the current cycle could not be verified live (Cloudflare interstitial) and the cached scrape shows zero benefits for all three target groups, which may be genuine (recent AGMs) or a scrape miss.

Methodology

This report cross-references three statutory registers, each operating under different rules and on different platforms. Each fact in the report is attributed to its source register and a link to the underlying API record is provided in the evidence drawer for that claim.

Selection rule for the twelve officers

Any APPG whose title or stated purpose (as recorded in the APPG register edition 260413) explicitly references artificial intelligence, machine learning, blockchain, web3, data and emerging technologies, or the AI/data sector specifically. This produces three groups: AI, Blockchain Technologies, and Data and Emerging Technologies. Their twelve officers (Chair, Co-Chair, Vice Chair, Officer; "Registered Contact" duplicates the Chair role) are the population. Adjacent groups whose remit touches AI more obliquely — e.g. Financial Technology, Quantum Technologies, ClimateTech — were not included; they could be a sensible expansion in a follow-up.

What this report does not do

  • It does not look at the wider APPG membership (only officers).
  • It does not extend the Lords-RMFI cross-reference to the other ~800 peers (no full-text search API, would need bulk download).
  • It does not attempt to disambiguate same-name donors via address — the EC register's default response omits the address field. Findings rely on consistent donor-status + recipient-party patterns and are flagged in the relevant evidence drawers.
  • It does not include party-level donations (e.g. Labour Party central donations from corporate donors) as "donations to" any individual officer.

Caveats

  1. APPG-level benefits in the cache are empty for all three target groups. This may be because the AGMs were recent and the financial schedule has not yet been required, or because the scraper missed the benefits section. A live re-fetch on 2026-05-24 was blocked by a Cloudflare interstitial on publications.parliament.uk. The absence of secretariat-funding figures in the cached scrape is not evidence that no such funding exists.
  2. APPG cache is 21 days old (fetched 2026-05-03, edition 260413; current edition may have moved on). The user invited use of the cache as it was; this report uses it.
  3. The Lords-RMFI free-text search is not implemented at the API layer. Anything found about a peer is per-peer (you ask their interests by id). There is no equivalent to the Commons CSV bundle for the Lords. We could not cheaply check whether any of the same payers (UKUS Crypto Alliance, Avalanche Foundation, etc.) appear in the records of peers outside our twelve.
  4. The Electoral Commission search wrapper in this repository is broken (lib/facilities/ec-donations.mjs) — the typed recipient and donorName parameters return Total: -1 on the live endpoint. Only the free-text query= parameter works, and only when sent with a full suite of entity-type and register flags. This needs a wrapper fix; in the meantime this report called the EC endpoint directly with the verified parameter set.
  5. EC free-text search hits any field of the record, not just the recipient. We filtered client-side so the table above lists only records where the named officer actually appears as recipient.
  6. Donor identity is inferred from name + donor-status + recipient-party context. The EC register's default API response does not surface the donor's address, so two people called "Anthony Watson" in different contexts could in principle be distinct humans. Where identity was inferred, this is flagged in the evidence drawer.
  7. Lords paid roles are not "donations". A Cat 1 entry is paid employment, not political support. This report treats those as commercial connections — arguably the more interesting question for an APPG officer — but the language matters.
  8. Edition cut-off: EC donations data covers everything published up to the run date (2026-05-24); RMFI entries are at register snapshot 799 (published 2026-05-18). Anything filed between the snapshot and the run is not in this report.
